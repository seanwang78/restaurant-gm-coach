# Brand GM Competency Framework (multi-brand restaurant group)

Use this framework to organise captured knowledge, to measure coverage of the coach, and to assess successors. Every knowledge card, exam scenario and assessment item carries one primary domain code (D1–D10) and optional cross-cutting tags (X1–X2).

A Brand GM (General Manager) owns one brand end to end: P&L (profit and loss), people, operations, guests, growth and partners. The job is broad. A successor is ready when they show Level 4 behaviour in most domains and Level 3 in all.

## Levels

| Level | Name (EN) | Typical role today | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | Runs a store | Store manager | Executes standards, manages one team and one store P&L line by line |
| L2 | Runs an area | Area / district manager | Gets results through several store managers, spots patterns across stores |
| L3 | Runs a function for the brand | Ops / marketing / brand manager | Owns one part of the brand (ops, marketing, menu, growth) and its numbers |
| L4 | Ready to run the brand | GM successor | Balances all domains, makes trade-offs, sets direction, accountable for full P&L |

## Domains

### D1 Strategy & brand direction
- Knows where the brand wins: target guests, occasions, price position, competitors.
- Turns the group's 3-year plan into a brand plan, annual budget and a few clear priorities.
- Says no to good ideas that do not fit the brand.
- L4 evidence: wrote or defended a brand annual plan; made a portfolio choice (stop, scale, reposition).

### D2 P&L & commercial
- Reads the P&L fast: sales = traffic (bills) × average check; prime cost = food + labour; occupancy; EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation).
- Knows the brand's normal ranges and what moves each line; finds the root cause behind a variance.
- Pricing decisions, discount depth, channel margin (dine-in vs delivery after commission).
- L4 evidence: owned a turnaround of a loss-making store or region; set prices or a promotion budget and showed the result.

### D3 Operations excellence
- Standards: QSC (quality, service, cleanliness), recipes, SOPs (standard operating procedures), audits.
- Peak management: staffing to demand, speed of service, kitchen flow, sold-outs.
- Store visits that change something: what to look at in the first 10 minutes.
- L4 evidence: lifted audit scores or speed across many stores; designed a new routine that stuck.

### D4 Guest experience & channels
- Dine-in, take-away, delivery aggregators, own app and website, loyalty members.
- Reviews and complaints: reading patterns, service recovery rules.
- L4 evidence: fixed a channel problem (late delivery, low rating) with data; grew members or own-channel share.

### D5 Marketing, menu & innovation
- Campaign calendar, LTO (limited time offer), menu engineering (stars, puzzles, plowhorses, dogs), new product launch.
- Measures real uplift, not just sales up; knows cannibalisation.
- L4 evidence: launched products or campaigns with measured incremental profit; killed one that did not work.

### D6 People & leadership
- Builds the team below: hiring, store manager pipeline, succession, coaching.
- Culture and engagement; turnover; incentives; local labour law basics.
- Hard conversations: performance, misconduct, restructuring.
- L4 evidence: developed people who were promoted; reduced turnover; handled a difficult people case well.

### D7 Growth & network
- New stores: site selection, format, investment case (capex, payback), opening plan.
- Refurbishments, relocations, closures; franchise partners where relevant.
- L4 evidence: opened or closed stores with a business case and reviewed the result.

### D8 Supply chain, cost & food safety
- Suppliers, central kitchen and DC (distribution centre), price negotiation, waste, inventory accuracy.
- Food safety is a locked area: brand and legal standards are never traded off.
- L4 evidence: reduced cost of goods without hurting quality; led a supply disruption response.

### D9 Partners & stakeholders
- Mall landlords, brand owners or franchisors (where the brand is licensed), aggregators, banks and payment partners, group functions, the executive committee and board.
- Negotiation, escalation, managing up.
- L4 evidence: negotiated rent or terms; aligned a brand owner on a local change; presented to the executive committee.

### D10 Risk, crisis & compliance
- Food safety incidents, social media crises, system outages, floods, protests, public holidays.
- Compliance: licences, labour law, data-protection law (e.g. GDPR, PDPA), tax.
- L4 evidence: led the response to a real incident; wrote or improved a crisis playbook.

## Cross-cutting tags

- **X1 Decision making**: how the GM weighs options, uses data, decides with incomplete information, and when to escalate.
- **X2 Data & AI mindset**: uses reports, forecasts and agents; asks the right questions of data; teaches the coach.
- **X3 Communication & influence**: explains decisions, gets buy-in from teams, partners and the executive committee, handles conflict.

## Using the framework

- **Talk**: conversations can jump across domains; classify each learning to one sub-skill. Suggest the thinnest sub-skills as next topics.
- **Synthesise**: domain summaries and the skill map are rebuilt from the knowledge (see `classify-and-synthesise.md`).
- **Exam**: every domain has scenarios; D8 food safety and D10 crisis scenarios must score 100%.
- **Mentor**: rate the successor L1–L4 per domain with evidence (a real example), never a gut score alone.
