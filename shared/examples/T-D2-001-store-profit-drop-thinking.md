## Situation
A mall store's evening sales were down 4 weeks in a row. The store manager asked to cut one person from the evening shift to protect labour %.

## Thinking step by step
1. Noticed: "Before I say yes to any cut, I split the drop: bills or average check?" → bills down ~12%, average check flat.
2. Meaning: "Flat check means the guests who come still buy the same. So it's not the menu or upselling — guests are not coming."
3. Checked: our two nearby stores and the mall footfall → nearby stores flat → "so it's this location, not the market."
4. Ruled out cutting staff: "If I cut now, the guests who still come wait longer, and next month I lose them too. That's how a slow store becomes a dead store."
5. Weighed: one month of extra labour cost vs losing the remaining evening guests → "labour I can fix in a week; lost guests take a year."
6. Would change my mind if: the cause is permanent (an office tower nearby emptied) and the drop lasts 3 months.

## Decision
No cut. Store manager checks for a new competitor and tests an evening offer for 4 weeks.

## What a less experienced manager would do
Cut the shift to hit labour %, because the report shows labour % going red. Service slows, the drop speeds up.
